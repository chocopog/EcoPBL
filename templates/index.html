Walk-forward performance

Strategy Final wealth Profit Total cost (TC) Trades Avg annual return Risk (std dev of return) Return per unit of risk Return per unit of downside risk Worst fall from peak Periods with profit Trading frequency Test splits

TR = gross gains; TC = cost per trade × trades; profit = TR − TC. Opportunity cost = what a safe asset would have earned. Risk = standard deviation of returns. Return per unit of risk = (average return − opportunity cost) / risk. Worst fall from peak = largest drop from the highest wealth so far. Hybrid = 75% always in the stock + 25% moved by the model. Test splits = train on the past, test on the next block of future days.

Figure 1: Market Price History & 50-Day Moving Average Equilibrium

Open High-Res
Economic Interpretation: The 50-day moving average represents a dynamic equilibrium price level. In accordance with mean-reversion theory, prices fluctuate around this moving benchmark as short-term market shocks resolve.

Figure 2: Daily Excess Demand (Shortage vs Surplus Pressure)

Open High-Res
Economic Interpretation: Green bars denote buying dominance (shortage, where volume on up-days outweighs down-days). Red bars denote selling dominance (surplus). According to the Law of Demand and Supply, persistent shortages create upward price pressure.

Figure 3: Microeconomic Supply & Demand Curve Model

Open High-Res
Economic Interpretation: Demonstrates the foundational textbook model: Consumer Surplus, Producer Surplus, Demand shifts from D to D2, and Price Elasticity of Demand/Supply calculated at market equilibrium \(E\).

Figure 4: LSTM Training Curve & Validation RMSE per Epoch

Open High-Res
ML Evaluation: Shows Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) across epochs. Early stopping halts training at the dashed vertical line to preserve generalization and avoid memorizing past historical noise.

Figure 5: Predicted vs Actual Test Trajectory (with 90-Day Zoom)

Open High-Res
Core Insight (Persistence vs Lag): While predicted prices track the overall path due to price inertia (\(P_{t+1} \approx P_t\)), the zoomed panel demonstrates that the model does not have a lookahead bug; daily return forecasts encounter the real-world efficiency boundary.

Figure 6: Residual Forecast Errors & Return Scatter Plot

Open High-Res
Statistical Validation: The left panel reveals error stationarity around zero. The right scatter plot inspects correlation with the 45° perfect prediction line and computes Directional Hit Rate.

Figure 7: Firm Economics — Total Revenue (\(TR\)) vs Total Cost (\(TC\))

Open High-Res
Firm Theory & Friction: Every transaction incurs a marginal trading fee (\(MC = 0.1\%\)). Profit (\(\pi = TR - TC\)) is positive only when Marginal Revenue (\(MR\)) exceeds transaction costs, demonstrating why excessive trading harms capital accumulation.

Figure 8: Investor Utility & Diminishing Marginal Utility (\(U(W) = \sqrt{W}\))

Open High-Res
Consumer Choice & Risk Aversion: The concave utility function (\(U''(W) < 0\)) mathematically proves why investors are risk-averse: losing \(X\) amount destroys more utility (well-being) than gaining the same \(X\) amount creates.
